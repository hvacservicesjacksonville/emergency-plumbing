What's included

What our toilet repair service includes

Every toilet repair visit starts with a full diagnosis of the tank, bowl, and supply line. We quote a flat price before we start, and the price covers the parts and labor for the repair we agree on. If we find a second problem, we tell you before we touch it.

01

Full tank and bowl inspection

We pull the lid, check the flapper, fill valve, flush valve, and overflow tube. We test the water level and watch for ghost flushing. If the bowl rocks, we check the wax ring and closet flange for cracks or corrosion.

02

Flapper and fill valve replacement

Worn flappers and fill valves are the number one cause of running toilets in Sioux Falls. We replace them with parts from Moen, Delta, Kohler, or American Standard and set the water level to the correct height for your toilet model.

03

Clog clearing and drain snaking

If the toilet is clogged, we start with a plunger, then move to a drain snake or auger if the blockage is past the trap. We clear paper, wipes, and scale. If the line keeps clogging, we recommend a sewer camera inspection.

04

Wax ring and flange repair

A toilet that leaks at the base needs a new wax ring, and often a flange repair ring. We pull the bowl, clean the old wax, inspect the flange, and reset the toilet with a new wax ring and new closet bolts. Most jobs take 45 to 60 minutes.

05

Supply line and shut-off valve check

We check the supply line and shut-off valve behind every toilet we service. A seized or weeping valve is a flood waiting to happen. We replace braided supply lines and quarter-turn shut-off valves while we are on site.

06

Testing and cleanup

We flush the toilet three to five times, check the base for moisture, and confirm the tank holds steady. We wipe down the bowl and floor, haul away the old parts, and leave you a written summary of what failed and what we replaced.

Tools & methods

Tools and methods we use

Purpose-built equipment, used by trained technicians, so the job is done accurately the first time.

01

Plunger and drain snake

First line for clogs. The plunger handles blockages in the trapway. A drain snake or auger clears paper, wipes, and scale past the trap and into the branch line.

02

Hydro jetter

For repeat clogs and grease or scale buildup in the drain line. The hydro jetter scours the pipe walls with high-pressure water and restores full flow. We use it when snaking only gives temporary relief.

03

Sewer camera

When a toilet backs up and we cannot see why, the sewer camera shows roots, cracks, bellies, and offset joints in the pipe. You get a clear answer instead of another temporary fix.

04

Pipe wrench and pipe cutter

Used to remove old supply lines, shut-off valves, and closet nuts that are corroded in place. The pipe cutter gives a clean cut on copper pipe or PEX tubing when we replace a section.

05

PEX crimping tool and soldering torch

For supply line repairs. We crimp PEX tubing with the crimping tool or solder copper pipe with the torch, flux, and solder. Both methods meet the Uniform Plumbing Code for potable water lines.

06

Pressure gauge

We check the water pressure at the supply line after any repair. Sioux Falls homes should sit between 40 and 80 psi. High pressure wears out fill valves and flappers faster.

07

Leak detection microphone

When a toilet leak is hidden under the floor or behind the wall, the microphone picks up the sound of moving water. We use it to find the leak before we open up the subfloor.

Problems we solve

Toilet problems we fix in Sioux Falls

Most toilet calls fall into four buckets: running, clogged, leaking, or dead. Here is what each one means and how we fix it.

ISSUE

Toilet runs nonstop and the water bill spikes

We replace the flapper and fill valve, set the water level to the correct mark, and confirm the tank holds with no trickle. Most running toilet repairs take 20 to 30 minutes. If the flush valve is also worn, we replace it in the same visit.

SYMPTOM

Toilet clogs every few weeks and plunging stops working

We run a drain snake or auger through the branch line to clear the blockage. If the clog keeps coming back, we bring in a hydro jetter or sewer camera to find roots or a cracked pipe. Clearing the line stops the repeat backups.

SIGN

Water pools at the base of the toilet

We pull the toilet, remove the old wax, and inspect the flange. If the flange is cracked, we install a repair ring. Then we set a new wax ring, reset the bowl, and test for leaks. Most base-leak repairs take 45 to 60 minutes.

ALERT

Toilet will not flush at all

We check the chain, flapper, and flush valve first. If the chain is broken, we replace it. If the flush valve is worn, we swap it. If the trapway is blocked, we run a drain snake. Most no-flush calls are fixed in one visit.

Type of repair

A flapper swap is the cheapest repair. A fill valve or flush valve replacement costs a bit more because the tank has to come off. A wax ring and flange repair costs the most because the toilet has to be pulled and reset.

Phase 02

Parts needed

Standard flappers and fill valves are inexpensive. Specialty parts for older or European toilets cost more and may need to be ordered. We tell you the part price before we install it.

Phase 03

Clog depth

A clog in the trapway is a quick fix. A clog in the branch line or main drain needs a drain snake, and a grease or root blockage may need a hydro jetter. The deeper the clog, the more time and equipment it takes.
