What Our Faucet Repair Service Includes

Every faucet repair call includes a full diagnosis, a flat price before work begins, and a test under full pressure after. 01

Full Faucet Diagnosis

We shut off the supply, open the fixture, and inspect the cartridge, O-rings, seals, and valve seat. We also check the aerator for scale and the supply lines for corrosion. You get a clear explanation of what failed and a flat price for the repair before we start.

02

Cartridge and Seal Replacement

Worn cartridges and hardened seals are the most common cause of drips. We match the replacement to the brand, install it with plumber's putty where needed, and reassemble the faucet. Most cartridges are on the truck, so the repair finishes in one visit.

Shut-Off Valve Inspection

The valves under your sink are the first line of defense against a flooded cabinet. We open and close them to check for weeping and corrosion. If a valve will not hold, we replace it while we are there. This is the step that prevents future emergencies.

04

Aerator Cleaning and Flush

Scale in the aerator screen cuts water flow and makes a faucet feel weak. We remove the aerator, clear the screen, and flush the line to remove loose debris. If the blockage is in the cartridge, we replace it. Most customers notice full pressure the same day.

05

Pressure Test and Cleanup

After reassembly, we run hot and cold water for several minutes and check every connection with a dry rag. If anything seeps, we reopen the faucet and fix it before we leave. Then we wipe down the sink, put the cabinet back, and haul away the old parts.

Tools & methods

Tools and Methods We Use

Purpose-built equipment, used by trained technicians, so the job is done accurately the first time.

01

Pipe Wrench

Used to hold supply lines and shut-off valves steady while we work on the faucet. A proper pipe wrench prevents twisting the valve body and cracking the solder joint behind the wall.

02

Pressure Gauge

We thread a pressure gauge onto a hose bibb or supply port to check household water pressure. High pressure above 80 psi stresses faucet cartridges and supply lines, so we test it on every call.

03

Teflon Tape

Wrapped on threaded supply connections to create a watertight seal. We use two to three wraps in the correct direction so the tape does not bunch when the fitting tightens.

04

Plumber's Putty

Seals the faucet base to the sink deck and the drain basket to the sink bowl. It stays pliable, so it seals without cracking like silicone can on a vibrating fixture.

05

PEX Crimping Tool

Used when we replace a corroded supply line with PEX tubing. The crimp ring makes a permanent, code-compliant connection that will not loosen like a compression fitting can.

06

Leak Detection Microphone

When a faucet repair reveals a hidden leak behind the wall, we use a leak detection microphone to pinpoint the source without opening the whole wall. It saves drywall and time.

Problems we solve

Faucet Problems We Fix in Sioux Falls

Faucet failures look different depending on the fixture and the water. Here are the problems we see most often on Sioux Falls calls, what happens if you ignore them, and how we fix each one.

ISSUE

Faucet drips constantly from the spout

We open the faucet and replace the worn cartridge or seat washer. In most cases the repair takes under an hour and stops the drip completely. We test under full pressure before we leave, so you know it is fixed.

SYMPTOM

Water pools at the base of the faucet

We pull the faucet, replace the O-rings and base gasket, and reseal the mounting with plumber's putty. We also check the cabinet floor for existing damage and the shut-off valves for corrosion. Most base leaks are a one-visit repair.

SIGN

Handle is stiff or spins without adjusting flow

We replace the cartridge or stem assembly and lubricate the moving parts with plumber's grease. If the handle itself is cracked, we source a replacement from Moen, Delta, or Kohler. The faucet should move smoothly through the full range again.

ALERT

Low water pressure at the faucet

We clean the aerator screen, flush the line, and check the cartridge for debris. If the supply line is corroded, we replace it with PEX tubing or a new braided line. Most low-pressure calls are resolved the same day.

Phase 01

Faucet type and brand

Single-handle cartridges are usually the fastest and cheapest to replace. Widespread faucets with three separate handles take longer because there are more seals and supply connections to check. Specialty finishes and European brands may need ordered parts.

Phase 02

Condition of shut-off valves

If the valves under the sink will not hold, we replace them during the repair. That adds parts and 20 to 30 minutes of labor. Skipping this step risks a flooded cabinet later, so we always recommend doing it while the water is off.

Phase 03

Corroded supply lines

Braided supply lines and older copper risers can corrode and restrict flow. Replacing them with new braided lines or PEX tubing adds a modest cost but restores full pressure and prevents future leaks at the connection.
