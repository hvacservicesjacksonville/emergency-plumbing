What's included

What Our Sump Pump Installation Includes

Every installation covers the pump, pit, discharge, and testing. We'll tell you upfront if your job needs something extra, like a new circuit or a backup system.

01

Pump and Basin

We supply a cast-iron or thermoplastic pump sized to your basement and water table, plus a perforated basin liner. We set the pump on a solid base so it doesn't vibrate or clog with sediment.

02

Discharge Line

We run PVC pipe from the pump to an approved discharge point, with a check valve to stop backflow. The line slopes to drain fully, and we add freeze protection so it doesn't ice up in a Sioux Falls January.

03

Electrical Connection

The pump ties into a dedicated GFCI outlet. If you don't have one, we can run a new circuit or tie into an existing one. We test the outlet under load before we call the job done.

04

Float and Switch Setup

We set the float height so the pump kicks on before water reaches your floor. We test the switch through several cycles and adjust it so it doesn't short-cycle or run dry.

05

Full System Test

We fill the pit with water and watch the pump run. We check the check valve, the discharge flow, and the backup if you have one. You'll see it work before we leave.

Our Sump Pump Installation Process

The process flexes to the condition, but these checkpoints keep decisions clear.

01

Call and Assess

You call +17136551138 or book online. We ask about your basement, whether you have an existing pit, and what's been happening. We schedule a visit, usually same-day for active water. The tech arrives with a pipe wrench, pressure gauge, and leak detection microphone to check for other issues while we're there.

Stage 1
02

Size the Pump

We measure your basement footprint, check the water table, and calculate head height from the pit to the discharge point. That tells us the gallons-per-minute and shutoff head you need. We carry Rheem, Zoeller, and Liberty pumps so we can match the right unit to your space.

Stage 2
03

Set the Pit and Pump

We cut the concrete, dig the basin, and set a perforated liner. The pump goes in with a check valve, and we plumb the discharge in PVC pipe with a freeze-resistant slope. A dedicated GFCI outlet powers the unit. This step takes 3 to 5 hours for a standard install.

Stage 3
04

Test and Adjust

We fill the pit with water and watch the pump cycle. We set the float height, confirm the check valve seals, and check that the discharge line drains completely. If you added a backup, we test that too. You'll see the pump run before we pack up.

Stage 4
05

Walkthrough and Cleanup

We show you how the system works, where the discharge exits, and what to check each season. We haul away the concrete and debris, wipe down the area, and leave you with a written summary of the install. Most customers are back to normal the same day.

Problems we solve

Problems We Solve With Sump Pump Installation

A failing sump pump shows up in a few predictable ways. Here's what we fix for Sioux Falls homeowners.

ISSUE

Basement floods every spring

We install a correctly sized pump and pit that handles spring snowmelt. We check your drain tile and discharge line, then test the system with water so you know it works before the next thaw.

SYMPTOM

Pump runs but doesn't move water

We diagnose the pump on site, clear the impeller, replace the check valve, and test flow. If the pump is too far gone, we install a new one the same day.

SIGN

Power goes out during a storm

We install battery backup or water-powered backup systems that run when the power is out. A battery backup gives you 6 to 8 hours of protection, enough for most Sioux Falls outages.

ALERT

Discharge line freezes in winter

We slope the discharge line to drain fully and add freeze protection. We also check that it exits above the frost line, so it keeps flowing through a South Dakota winter.

Phase 01

New Pit vs. Existing

If you already have a pit, we can often swap the pump in 2 hours. Cutting a new pit means concrete work, digging, and a liner, which adds 2 to 3 hours and $200 to $400.

Phase 02

Pump Size and Type

A 1/3 HP pump costs less than a 3/4 HP cast-iron unit. Submersible pumps cost more than pedestal models but run quieter and handle solids better. We'll match the pump to your water table.

Phase 03

Backup System

A battery backup adds $250 to $500 installed. A water-powered backup runs $150 to $350. Both protect you during power outages, and we recommend one if your basement is finished.
