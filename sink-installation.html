What's included

What our sink installation includes

Every sink install starts with removal of the old basin and ends with a pressure test. What we include depends on the sink type and the condition of your existing plumbing. Here is what is standard on a typical job, and what we quote separately if we find it.

01

Old sink removal

We disconnect the faucet, drain, and disposal, then cut through old caulk and putty to lift the old basin out. We scrape the countertop clean and check the cutout for cracks or soft spots. The old sink goes in the truck unless you want to keep it.

02

New sink setting and sealing

We set your new drop-in, undermount, or farmhouse sink into the opening, check level both ways, and seal with the correct product for your counter material. Undermount sinks get new mounting clips or epoxy anchors rated for the weight.

03

Drain and trap rebuild

New basket strainer, new tailpiece, and a P-trap set to code slope. We check the trap arm and vent so the sink drains fast and sewer gas stays out. If the old drain was S-trapped, we correct it while the cabinet is open.

04

Supply line and valve check

We connect new braided supply lines and test your shut-off valves. If a valve will not close or is weeping, we replace it. That is the number one cause of a second leak after a sink install, and we would rather fix it now.

05

Pressure test and cleanup

We fill the basin, pull the stopper, and watch every joint under full pressure for at least five minutes. Any weep gets fixed before we pack up. Then we wipe down the counter, haul away debris, and show you the shut-off location.

Tools & methods

Tools and methods we use

Purpose-built equipment, used by trained technicians, so the job is done accurately the first time.

01

Pipe wrench and basin wrench

For loosening supply line nuts and faucet mounting nuts in tight cabinet spaces. A basin wrench reaches where a regular wrench cannot, so we do not force anything and crack the sink.

02

Pipe cutter

For clean cuts on copper pipe and PEX tubing when we replace shut-off valves or repair a damaged stub-out. A square cut means a better solder joint or crimp connection.

03

Soldering torch and PEX crimping tool

For copper pipe repairs we sweat a new fitting with solder and flux. For PEX tubing we use a crimping tool and the correct rings. Both methods meet South Dakota plumbing code.

04

Drain snake and hydro jetter

For clearing the branch line before a new sink goes in. A hand snake handles most kitchen clogs. A hydro jetter scours grease and scale from the pipe walls so the drain stays open longer.

05

Sewer camera

For checking the main line when a sink backs up repeatedly. The camera shows roots, breaks, or a belly in the pipe. We show you the video before we recommend a repair.

06

Pressure gauge

For checking household water pressure before we connect a new faucet. High pressure wears out cartridges and supply lines fast. If it is over 80 psi, we recommend a pressure-reducing valve.

Problems we solve

Sink problems we solve for Sioux Falls homeowners

Most sink calls start with water where it should not be. Here are the problems we see most often and how we fix them.

ISSUE

Water pooling in the cabinet under the sink

We find the source with a dry paper towel test on every joint, then repair or replace the failed part. If the cabinet floor is soft, we tell you before we cover it up. We pressure-test after the fix so you know it is dry.

SYMPTOM

Faucet base corroded into the sink deck

We cut the faucet nuts from below with a mini hacksaw or use a basin wrench with the right leverage. If the sink is sound, we save it. If the deck is cracked, we quote a new sink and install it the same visit.

SIGN

Slow drain that backs up after a new sink goes in

We run a drain snake or hydro jetter through the branch line while the cabinet is open. If the clog is deeper, we use a sewer camera to see the main line. We clear it first, then finish the sink install.

ALERT

Shut-off valves that will not close

We replace angle stops with new quarter-turn ball valves in copper pipe or PEX tubing. That gives you a shut-off that actually works. We test it before we connect the new supply lines.

Phase 01

Sink type and mounting

A drop-in sink is the simplest and cheapest to install. Undermount sinks require clips or epoxy anchors and more careful sealing, which adds labor. Farmhouse and apron-front sinks need cutout adjustments and extra support, so they cost more.

Phase 02

Countertop material

Laminate and solid-surface tops are easy to work with. Granite, quartz, and quartzite need diamond blades or existing cutouts, and we take extra care not to chip the edge. If we have to enlarge a cutout, that adds time.

Phase 03

Condition of shut-off valves

If your angle stops close and reopen cleanly, no charge. If they are frozen or weeping, we replace them with new quarter-turn ball valves. That is a small part cost plus labor, and it prevents a flood later.
