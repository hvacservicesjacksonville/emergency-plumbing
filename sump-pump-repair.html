What's included

What our sump pump repair includes

Every repair visit covers the full pit, not just the part that failed. We diagnose the whole system so you are not calling us back next month for the next weak link. Here is what is included in a standard repair.

01

Full float switch testing

We test the switch by hand and with a multimeter, check the tether for snags, and replace it with an OEM-matched part if it fails. This is the most common sump pump failure point.

02

Intake screen cleaning

Sioux Falls clay silt packs the intake screen and starves the pump. We pull the pump, flush the screen, and clear gravel and debris from the pit bottom so water flows freely.

03

Check valve inspection

A failed check valve lets water drain back into the pit and short-cycles the pump. We inspect the flapper, test the seal, and replace the valve if it is stuck or leaking.

04

Discharge line check

We verify the discharge line slopes away from the foundation, check for freeze damage, and confirm it terminates outside, not into the sanitary sewer. Code compliance matters here.

05

Full cycle test

After the repair, we fill the pit with a hose and watch a complete pump cycle. If the pump does not kick on and drain the pit, we keep working. You see it running before we leave.

Problems we solve

Sump pump problems we fix in Sioux Falls

Most sump pump failures fall into a handful of categories. Here is what we see most often and what happens if you let it ride.

ISSUE

Float switch stuck or burned out

We replace the switch with an OEM-matched part, test it by hand and with a multimeter, and confirm a full pump cycle. Most float switch repairs take 60 to 90 minutes and carry a one-year warranty.

SYMPTOM

Clogged intake screen from clay silt

We pull the pump, flush the screen, clear gravel from the pit bottom, and test flow rate. If the motor is already damaged, we quote a replacement before doing any work.

SIGN

Failed check valve recycling water

We inspect the flapper, replace the check valve with a matched PVC fitting, and verify the discharge line holds pressure. This stops the short-cycling and extends pump life.

ALERT

Frozen or blocked discharge line

We thaw or reroute the line with proper slope and insulation, confirm it terminates away from the foundation, and test a full cycle. We also check for code violations on the termination point.

Why is my sump pump not working in Sioux Falls South Dakota?

The three most common causes we find are a stuck or burned-out float switch, a clogged intake screen packed with clay silt, and a seized impeller from running dry. Sioux Falls clay soil sends fine sediment into pits, and that grit kills pumps fast. A tripped GFCI outlet or a frozen discharge line in January are the next two culprits.

Can you repair a sump pump float switch in Sioux Falls?

Yes. Float switch repair is our most frequent sump pump job. We test the switch with a multimeter, confirm whether the tether is hung up on the pit wall or the diaphragm has failed, and replace it with an OEM-matched part. Most float switch repairs take 60 to 90 minutes and carry a one-year workmanship warranty.
