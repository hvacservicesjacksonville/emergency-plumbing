What Our Water Softener Repair Includes

Every repair call includes diagnosis, the fix, and a full test before we leave. We tell you upfront what's covered and what would be a separate job, like a full unit replacement or new drain line.

01

Full System Diagnosis

We test incoming hardness, check line pressure with a pressure gauge, and inspect the control valve, resin tank, brine tank, and bypass. You get a clear explanation of what failed and why, not a vague guess.

02

Valve and Piston Repair

Worn pistons, seals, and control valves are the most common failure points. We rebuild or replace them with matched parts for Rheem, A.O. Smith, and other major brands, then cycle the unit to confirm it moves through each stage.

03

Brine Tank and Salt Service

We break up salt bridges, clear clogged brine lines, and clean the venturi. If your salt isn't dropping, this is usually the fix. We also set the salt dose and hardness number for Sioux Falls water.

04

Resin Rebed or Clean

When resin is fouled by iron or sediment, softening stops even if the valve works. We can rebed or clean the tank so the ion exchange does its job again. We test output hardness to prove it.

05

Leak and Connection Check

We inspect supply lines, shut-off valves, and threaded connections for leaks. Teflon tape and plumber's putty go on any joint we open, and we pressure-test everything before we call the job done.

06

Post-Repair Test and Walkthrough

We run a full regeneration cycle, test the water, and show you the results. You also get a quick lesson on salt levels and how to spot a salt bridge early so you're not calling us again next month.

Problems we solve

Softener Problems We Fix in Sioux Falls

Most softener failures fall into a handful of patterns. Here's what we see most in Sioux Falls homes and how we fix each one.

ISSUE

Softener not regenerating or stuck mid-cycle

We test the control valve, timer, and piston, then rebuild or replace the failed part. After a full cycle test, we recalibrate the salt dose and hardness for Sioux Falls water so it regenerates on schedule.

SYMPTOM

Salt bridge or mushy salt in the brine tank

We break up the bridge, clean the tank, and clear the clogged brine line and venturi. Then we set the correct salt level and show you how to check for bridging before it becomes a full breakdown.

SIGN

Water softener leaking onto the floor

We find the source, whether it's a cracked fitting, failed O-ring, or split brine line, and repair it with matched parts. We pressure-test the unit and check nearby shut-off valves so the leak is truly fixed.

ALERT

Softener runs but water is still hard

We test hardness at the tap, then check the resin bed, bypass valve, and programming. Often the unit is stuck in bypass or the resin is fouled. We fix the cause and verify soft water before we leave.

How much does water softener repair cost in Sioux Falls SD?

Most water softener repairs in Sioux Falls run $180 to $450 in 2026, depending on the part and brand. A clogged venturi or brine line cleanout sits at the low end. A control valve rebuild or resin rebed pushes toward the top. We quote a range on the phone and confirm it on-site before any work starts.

Why is my water softener not working in Sioux Falls South Dakota?

The most common causes are a salt bridge in the brine tank, a clogged venturi, a worn piston seal, or a control board that failed after a power surge. Sioux Falls water is hard enough that resin can foul early too. We test hardness and cycle the unit to find the real fault instead of guessing.

Can you repair a water softener in Sioux Falls?

Yes. We repair all major brands, including Rheem, A.O. Smith, and salt-free systems, and we carry common parts on the truck. Most repairs finish the same day. If your unit is older and parts are discontinued, we'll tell you honestly whether a repair or a new install makes more sense.

How often should water softeners be serviced in Sioux Falls SD?

Once a year is right for most Sioux Falls homes because of how hard the water is. We clean the venturi, check the resin, test hardness, and recalibrate the salt dose. Homes on well water or with high iron may need service every six to nine months. Annual service prevents most emergency breakdowns.
