How We Execute Plumbing Work in Cathedral Historic District

Plumbing work in Cathedral Historic District runs on a different set of rules than a new subdivision. The streets around St. Joseph Cathedral are narrow, parking is tight, and many basements are accessed through bulkhead

01

Narrow streets limit equipment access

Full-size sewer jetters and trenching equipment cannot always reach the side yards of homes near the Cathedral. That can slow down a sewer line replacement or a repipe if we have to hand-dig. How we help: We use compact hydro jetters and mini-excavators that fit through a standard gate. For tight spots, we hand-dig the trench and keep the footprint small. We also schedule those jobs for mornings when street parking is easier.

02

Original galvanized and copper pipe corrodes

Homes built before 1930 in Cathedral Historic District often have galvanized steel supply lines that rust from the inside. That causes low water pressure, brown water, and pinhole leaks behind walls. How we help: We scope the system with a pressure gauge and a camera to see how much pipe is affected. If it is a small section, we repair it with copper or PEX. If the whole house is galvanized, we recommend a full repipe with PEX tubing.

03

Cold winters increase frozen pipe risk

Sioux Falls winters drop below zero, and Cathedral District homes with pipes in exterior walls or unheated crawlspaces freeze fast. A frozen line can burst and flood a basement in hours. How we help: We insulate exposed pipes, add heat tape where needed, and install shut-off valves so you can isolate a burst section. If a pipe does freeze, we thaw it with a controlled heat source, not an open torch.
