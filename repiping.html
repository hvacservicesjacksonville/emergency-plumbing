What's included

What a Full Repipe Includes

A repipe is a package, not an hourly job. Here is what is covered on a standard whole-home replacement, and where add-ons like fixture swaps or new hose bibs come in.

01

New Supply Lines Throughout

Every hot and cold supply line from the main shut-off to each fixture gets replaced in copper or PEX. No abandoned runs, no capped stubs left in the wall. We count fixtures during the walkthrough so the quote matches the work.

02

New Shut-Off Valves

Every fixture gets a new quarter-turn shut-off valve, and we replace the main valve at the meter. Old gate valves seize shut, which is what turns a small leak into a flooded floor. New valves mean you can isolate any fixture in seconds.

03

Permit and Inspection

We pull the repiping permit through the City of Sioux Falls and meet the inspector for the rough-in sign-off. You get the permit record for your files, which matters at resale and for insurance.

04

Pressure Test Documentation

The new system is charged to 100 PSI and held for at least one hour. We record the start and end readings and leave you the results. If the gauge moves, we find the leak before anything gets closed up.

05

Access Openings and Patch

We open the minimum wall needed, usually at fixture backs and a few ceiling spots. Openings get patched and ready for paint. We do not do finish painting, but we leave you smooth, sanded drywall.

06

System Flush and Walkthrough

We flush every line until the water runs clear, then walk the house with you to show the new valves, the main shut-off, and the pressure test results. You get a labeled system you can actually operate.

Service overview

What Repiping Sioux Falls SD Actually Involves

Repiping means pulling out every supply line in the house and running new pipe from the main shut-off to each fixture. We cut out the old galvanized or copper with a pipe cutter, run new copper pipe or PEX tubing through the walls, ceiling, and basement, and tie it in with new shut-off valves and pipe fittings. Nothing gets capped and abandoned. Every run is soldered or crimped, then pressurized with a gauge for at least an hour before the drywall goes back up.

Tools & methods

Tools and Methods We Use

Purpose-built equipment, used by trained technicians, so the job is done accurately the first time.

01

PEX Crimping Tool

Makes the ASTM-rated crimp connections on every PEX fitting. We calibrate the tool daily and pull a test crimp on each job to confirm the connection holds at pressure.

02

Soldering Torch

Used for copper repipes on every joint. We sweat fittings with lead-free solder and flux, then wipe and inspect each joint before the pressure test.

03

Pipe Cutter

Cuts copper and PEX cleanly so there is no burr or deformation at the fitting. Clean cuts are what keep crimp and solder joints from weeping later.

04

Pressure Gauge

Charges the new system to 100 PSI and holds it for at least an hour. The gauge reading is our proof the whole system is leak-free before walls close.

05

Leak Detection Microphone

Finds hidden leaks behind walls and under slabs before we quote a repipe. It tells us whether you have one bad run or a system-wide problem.

06

RIDGID Press Tool

Mechanical press connections for copper where open flame is not safe, like near joists or in tight chases. Same pressure rating as a soldered joint.

Problems we solve

Problems a Repipe Puts Behind You

Most repipe calls start with a symptom the homeowner has been living with for years. Here are the four we hear most in Sioux Falls, and what each one actually means for your pipe.

ISSUE

Pinhole leaks that keep coming back in new spots

We pressure-isolate the system with a gauge to confirm the whole run is compromised, then quote a full replacement. New copper or PEX ends the cycle instead of resetting the clock on the next pinhole.

SYMPTOM

Brown or rusty water after a weekend away

We cut a section and show you the interior scale so you can see the problem. Repiping removes every corroded run and restores clear water at every tap within a day of flushing.

SIGN

Low water pressure when two fixtures run at once

We measure static and dynamic pressure at the hose bib and at the furthest fixture. After repiping, most homes gain 15 to 25 PSI at the second floor and stop fighting over flow.

ALERT

Frozen pipes that burst in a January cold snap

We repipe with PEX where runs sit in exterior walls, since it flexes instead of splitting. We also relocate vulnerable runs and add insulation so the next cold snap is not an emergency.

Pricing process

What Repiping Costs in Sioux Falls

$4,500 - $12,000 for a whole-home repipe; $2,200+ for partial branch-line replacement

Phase 01

Fixture Count

Every toilet, sink, shower, tub, and hose bib is a separate run. A one-bath ranch has roughly eight supply points; a three-bath two-story with a wet bar can hit twenty. Fixture count moves the price more than square footage does.

Phase 02

Pipe Material

PEX tubing and fittings cost less than copper, and it installs faster, which is why it comes in 20 to 30 percent lower on the same layout. Copper lasts longer in open basement runs and some homeowners prefer it for resale documentation.

Phase 03

Access Difficulty

Finished basements, plaster walls, and tile showers all add labor. We plan access points to open the least wall possible, but a 1920s Cathedral Historic District home takes longer than a 1990s Willow Ridge build with open cavities.

How much does repiping cost in Sioux Falls SD?

Typical 2026 pricing in Sioux Falls runs $4,500 to $12,000 for a whole house, with most single-story homes landing between $6,000 and $8,500. Fixture count drives it more than square footage. A three-bedroom ranch with one full bath sits at the low end; a two-story with three baths, a basement bar sink, and two hose bibs pushes toward the top. PEX comes in roughly 20 to 30 percent under copper.

How long does whole home repiping take in Sioux Falls South Dakota?

Plan on two to four working days for most houses. Day one is rough-in, day two is the pressure test and city inspection, and days three and four cover fixture reconnects, patching, and flushing. Larger two-story homes with finished basements sometimes run five days. Your water is off during working hours only, and we turn it back on each evening so you can shower and do laundry.

Is PEX or copper better for repiping in Sioux Falls?

Both pass Sioux Falls code, and the right answer depends on your house. PEX handles our freeze-thaw swings better, installs faster, and costs less, which is why about 70 percent of our repipes use it. Copper lasts longer in open basement runs and some homeowners prefer it for resale. We quote both so you can compare on price and lifespan instead of guesswork.

How do I know if my Sioux Falls home needs repiping?

Three or more leaks in two years, brown water after a weekend away, or pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once all point to failing pipe. Galvanized steel in pre-1960 homes is past its service life at 50 to 70 years. If a plumber has already patched the same system twice, call for a repipe estimate before the next leak finds your ceiling.
