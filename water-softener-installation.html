What's included

What's Included in a Standard Install

Every water softener installation we do includes the essentials: the unit, the bypass valve, the drain line, the tie-in, programming, and cleanup. We quote the full job upfront so there are no add-ons later. If your home needs extra work, like a new drain or a pipe repair near the tie-in, we show you the problem and quote it before we proceed.

01

Softener Unit and Resin Tank

We supply a correctly sized system from Rheem, A.O. Smith, or Bradford White based on your water test and household size. The resin tank, brine tank, and control head come as a matched set. We don't mix brands or use refurbished tanks.

02

Bypass Valve Installation

A bypass valve lets you isolate the softener for service without shutting off water to the whole house. We install a quality brass or stainless valve on every job. It's a small part that saves you a headache later.

03

Drain Line to Code

The regeneration backwash needs a proper drain. We run the line to a floor drain, laundry standpipe, or sump pit, with the correct air gap to meet Uniform Plumbing Code. No dumping on the floor, no makeshift hose into a bucket.

04

Main Line Tie-In

We cut into the incoming water line and tie in the softener with copper or PEX tubing, using the right fittings and Teflon tape. The connection is pressure-tested before we close up. You get soft water to every fixture downstream.

05

Programming and Walkthrough

We set the control head for your water hardness and household size, then run a manual regeneration to confirm the drain and brine draw work. You get a walkthrough on salt levels, regeneration frequency, and what the display means. No guesswork.

06

Cleanup and Old Unit Haul-Away

We haul away the old softener and any packaging, wipe down the area, and test the water to confirm hardness is down to 1 to 3 grains per gallon. You get a follow-up call in two weeks to make sure everything's dialed in.

The service timeline

Our Water Softener Install Process

The process flexes to the condition, but these checkpoints keep decisions clear.

01

Water Test

We test your water on site for hardness, iron, and pH. Sioux Falls water typically runs 17 to 20 grains per gallon, but iron levels vary by neighborhood and well depth. That test tells us whether you need a standard softener or one with an iron filter. Takes about 15 minutes.

Stage 1
02

System Sizing

We size the unit to your household, not a generic chart. A family of four with a 50-gallon water heater usually needs a 40,000 to 48,000 grain system. We factor in how many bathrooms you run, whether you have a high-efficiency washer, and how often you'll be regenerating. Oversized units waste salt; undersized ones run out of capacity mid-week.

Stage 2
03

Installation Day

We shut off the main, cut into the incoming line, and install a bypass valve so you can still get water if the unit needs service. The resin tank and brine tank go in, the drain line runs to a floor drain or laundry standpipe, and we solder or crimp the connections clean. Three to five hours, and we protect your floors while we work.

Stage 3
04

Programming and Calibration

We set the control head for your water hardness and household size, then run a manual regeneration cycle to confirm the drain flows properly and the brine draw works. You get a walkthrough on salt levels, regeneration frequency, and what the display means. We don't leave until you understand how to run it.

Stage 4
05

Cleanup and Follow-Up

We haul away the old unit, wipe down the area, and test the water again to confirm hardness is down to 1 to 3 grains per gallon. You get a call two weeks later to make sure everything's dialed in. If the regeneration cycle needs tweaking, we come back and adjust it at no charge.

Tools and Methods We Use

Purpose-built equipment, used by trained technicians, so the job is done accurately the first time.

01

Pipe Cutter and PEX Crimping Tool

For clean, square cuts on copper pipe and PEX tubing, and for crimping rings to code spec. A proper crimp prevents leaks at the tie-in.

Soldering Torch, Solder, and Flux

For copper tie-ins where code or existing material calls for soldered joints. We use lead-free solder and the right flux for a watertight connection.

Pressure Gauge

To test the incoming line pressure and confirm the system holds after the tie-in. Sioux Falls municipal pressure typically runs 50 to 70 psi.

To measure grains per gallon and iron levels on site. That test drives the sizing decision, so we're not guessing at capacity.

For sealing threaded fittings and setting drain assemblies. Small materials that prevent big leaks behind the tank.
